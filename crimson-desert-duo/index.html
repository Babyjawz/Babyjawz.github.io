How to play Duo

Duo is a gambling game in Crimson Desert. Each player receives two numbered sticks. Your aim is to finish the round with the highest-ranked hand and win the pot.

01

Read the sticks

Each stick has a number shown using tally marks. The sticks appear in ochre-gold and red, and the colours matter for certain special hands.

02

Check your hand

Strong combinations beat ordinary point totals. Learn the order: premium combinations, matching pairs, named combinations, then ordinary points.

03

Decide whether to bet

Compare your hand with the sticks you can see opponents holding. Depending on the betting round, you can check, call, raise, or fold.

04

Reveal and compare

When the betting ends, the best-ranked hand wins. Unusual special combinations can change the normal result or cause a rematch.

Cheating and catching cheaters

In Duo, both you and the NPCs can cheat. Pay attention to how the dealer takes a stick from the cup — the animation can give away a dishonest draw.

Normal draw

The dealer generally raises their elbow and draws from the top of the cup, with a more natural downward hand position.

Suspicious draw

Watch for a tucked-in or lowered elbow, a stick taken from the side of the cup, or a thumb-up, ice-pick-like grip. Look at the whole movement rather than one still frame.

Accusing a player

Use the game's Accuse action when you have seen a suspicious deal. A correct accusation can remove the cheater and award or redistribute their Silver. A false accusation risks losing money and being thrown out or temporarily barred from the table.

Learning to cheat yourself

Watch NPCs cheating and use the Watch and Learn interaction when it becomes available. After learning the technique, you can use the game's Hide Hand cheating action while dealing to influence your stick selection. Follow the on-screen prompts — the opportunities depend on the stage of the game.

Remember: A strong opposing hand does not prove cheating. Watch the arm, thumb and direction of the draw, then decide whether to accuse.

How ordinary points work

Add the two numbers and keep the last digit. For example, 7 + 8 = 15, so that hand is worth 5 points. But check for pairs or named hands before calculating points.
